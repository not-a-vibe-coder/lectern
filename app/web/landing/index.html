The problem with class WhatsApp groups

Past questions get buried under four hundred messages. Lecture notes sit on one laptop. The night before the exam, everyone asks for the same PDF. Lectern gives your class one shelf, sorted by course code.

Three steps. That's the whole setup.

Create, upload, share. Most reps finish in one lecture break.

  1. 01 Create a shelf. Name it after your class, like FUTO · Computer Science · 300 Level. Sign in with your Sui wallet and the shelf is yours on any device.

  2. 02 Upload once. Add PDFs, slides, Word files and photos of past questions. Tag each one with a course code, type and semester.

  3. 03 Share one link. Post it in the class WhatsApp group, or print the QR poster for the lecture hall.

Questions, answered.

Do my classmates need an account or a wallet?

No. Only the rep who manages the shelf signs in. Students just open the link.

How much data does it use?

The first visit loads the app, about 3.5 MB, and your browser keeps it for next time. After that a shelf is a small list. Files download only when you open them.

What files can I upload?

PDF, PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, Word (.docx) and PowerPoint (.pptx), up to 25 MB each. Older .doc and .ppt files need to be saved as .docx or .pptx first.

What is Walrus?

A decentralised storage network. Lectern stores each file there for a set period and shows when that period ends.

What happens when a file's storage ends?

The file stays listed with a "Storage ended" tag so nobody wonders where it went, and the rep can upload it again.

Can I take a file down?

Yes. Hiding a file removes it from the shelf at once. The stored copy stays on Walrus until its storage period ends. It is owned by your Sui address, so you can also delete it early with Walrus tools.

Why do reps sign in with a wallet?

It proves the shelf is yours, so you can manage it from any phone or laptop without passwords or secret links. Your wallet also pays for storage when you upload, so the files belong to your address and to nobody else.

Is it free?

Lectern is free and open source under the MIT licence, and students pay nothing. Storing files on Walrus costs a little WAL, paid by the rep's wallet when uploading, plus a little SUI for gas. The app shows the cost before your wallet asks you to approve. Slush can swap SUI for WAL.
